I interviewed for two jobs a year apart — one in the summer of 2024, one in the summer of 2025. Both times I started from a cold company search and ended at an offer, and both times I forgot almost immediately how much work went into it.

It turns out five separate data trails survive: exports from the two AI tools I was using, plus my browsing, YouTube and Google Search history. Between them they account for at least 106 hours of prep. Each one shows a part of the work the others miss.

Every number here is a floor, and one trail is missing entirely. 2024 — Omnea

Six weeks, 49.8 hours, entirely on Claude. What survives beyond the AI export is search and YouTube.

  • Fri 2 Aug, 16:43
    Cold research, one sitting — and the AI got there first.

    The hunt opens on Claude: "What is hasura", then "Can you explain what the company omnea does and any competitors" sixteen minutes later. Google follows at 17:26. A year later that order reverses.

  • Tue 6 Aug — 4.6 h
    The single longest session in either export.

    3h 19m on TypeScript module interfaces. The first fortnight was all language and data: namespaces, enums, runtime type checking, MongoDB aggregation pipelines.

  • Wed 14 Aug
    A second process running in parallel.

    "am interviewing at [OTHER COMPANY] do you have an example of the coding test they use" — two companies at once, which is part of why this window is so much heavier.

  • Thu 22 Aug, 22:38–22:46
    Five answers to the same question in eight minutes.

    "Design Spotify", watched back to back from five different channels. Not watching a video; triangulating an answer.

  • 20 Aug – 9 Sep
    Three weeks of system design, from scratch — and not only on Claude.

    Kafka, Elasticsearch, Spark, Flink, consistent hashing, CDNs, TLS, red-black trees, "Design Twitter's feed". Running alongside: 69 videos over 17 days.

  • 26 – 30 Aug
    I studied a conference talk by one of my interviewers.

    Twelve searches for that name across the hunt, one of them "[INTERVIEWER A] talks". Their conference talk turns up in my watch history three times, and gets saved to a playlist. Search, watch, save.

  • Wed 11 Sep, 11:16
    Offer stage: I priced the company myself.

    Pasted their Series A share allotment filing into Claude and worked out the valuation from it.

  • Wed 11 Sep, 12:07–12:14
    Then six searches in seven minutes.

    Three variations on one interviewer's name, then the investor, then another interviewer, then Crunchbase. The people and the cap table in one sitting, fifty-one minutes after the valuation question. Prep activity stops the same day.

2025 — Skiller Whale

Four weeks, 28.1 hours of AI plus 28.2 hours of browsing, and the only hunt where all five trails survive. Two clear pushes: the first week was language — I'd been writing TypeScript and the role was Ruby on Rails — and the second, three weeks later, was infrastructure, triggered by the take-home landing on 8 September.

  • Sat 23 Aug, 07:53
    It starts in the browser, one minute before the first prompt.

    07:53 — Google the company, their site, the job posting. 07:54 — the ad goes to Claude. Fifty-three minutes on the role and what to cover, then straight into Terraform and ECS from a standing start.

  • Sun 24 Aug, 12:06
    I looked up who I'd be working for, then rewrote my own profile.

    LinkedIn: the founder, then an engineer. An hour later my own profile, five times in ten minutes. At 13:25 the application form opened.

  • Mon 25 Aug, 21:05
    Applied.

    Two days and roughly three hours of research after first sight of the ad.

  • Tue 26 Aug, 20:34
    Due diligence: I pulled their Companies House filing.

    Downloaded the statutory accounts and had them read line by line. Either side of it: the funding coverage, Crunchbase, their investor's portfolio page, and two of the team looked up by name.

  • Wed 27 Aug — 3.0 h
    A Ruby and Rails crash course in one day, then a second opinion at 21:50.

    Seven Claude conversations on the language, Rails 8, naming conventions, the request lifecycle, Redis. Then, late that night, a 49-message ChatGPT session on the same problem from the infrastructure side.

  • Fri 29 Aug, 12:10
    "how to pronounce [FOUNDER]"

    Six days after seeing the ad, three weeks before meeting them.

  • Sun 31 Aug, 14:38
    I knew what the technical interview would be, and rehearsed it.

    "I'm going to do a coding interview. It will be about spinning up temporary EC2 instances in the cloud that can be used as virtual coding sandboxes…"

  • Mon 8 Sep — 4.1 h
    The task landed. I put it to both tools, eight hours apart.

    Claude at 13:43, ChatGPT at 21:22 — the same brief, asked the same way, then compared.

  • Tue 9 Sep — 5.1 h
    The heaviest day in either hunt.

    Terraform and CIDR blocks in the morning; from 16:37 a single 89-message, 2h 38m session learning Mermaid from scratch to draw the architecture; then an 89-message voice conversation on dynamic scaling — talking it through out loud rather than typing. Plus ten tutorials in an afternoon.

  • Wed 10 – Thu 11 Sep — 7.6 h
    Twenty-seven conversations in two days, stress-testing the design.

    Network isolation, security groups, NAT gateways, EFS, Packer AMI builds, ALB vs API Gateway — and, in the middle of it, modelling the domain in Rails.

  • Fri 12 Sep
    Half an hour before the presentation, then the AI trail stops dead.

    Redis for polling EC2 status at 06:43, NAT gateway subnet placement at 10:12. No further AI prep at all.

  • Sat 13 – Mon 15 Sep — 3.1 h browsing
    But the browser kept going — this is the offer stage.

    The founder's entire Medium archive, read in order, then the company podcast, the LinkedIn people page, and their profile down to the patents. Zero AI conversations in the same period.

How this was measured

Built from three personal data exports: Claude (1,127 conversations), ChatGPT (688 conversations) and Google Takeout (Chrome history, Search, YouTube). Engaged time is the gaps between my own events, capped at 15 minutes each, plus 3 minutes of reading per AI conversation. Something counts as prep if it falls inside the window and is substantially about that role rather than day-job work: 128 of 173 conversations in 2024, 77 of 114 in 2025, and 729 of 6,217 page visits. YouTube and Search are counts only — Takeout records that a video was opened, not for how long — so neither contributes hours. The 2024 hunt has no browsing record at all: Chrome retains twelve months, local profiles about 90 days, and no backup goes further back.
